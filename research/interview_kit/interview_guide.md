# R1 Item 4 — Interview guide (with timings)

25 minutes total. Questions in exact order; add only the probes shown.
The friend-interviewer reads `interviewer_script_friend.md`; this is the researcher's master copy.

## A. Context (2 min)
- "How long have you used Google Photos? Roughly how many photos? Android or iPhone? Is backup automatic?"
- Log: years_of_use, library_size_est, device, auto_backup → segment_flags.

## B. Critical incident (5 min)
1. "Think about the last time you went looking for an old photo, video or screenshot that you knew existed. What were you trying to find?"
2. "Why did you need it at that moment?"
3. "What did you remember about it?"
   - probe (once): "How sure were you about each thing?"
   - log every cue: type | value | certainty (sure / think so)
4. "What did you not remember that you wished you did?"
5. "What was the first thing you typed or tapped?"
6. "What happened next?"  *(follow-ups: "Then what did you do?" — do not suggest)*
7. "How did it end?"  → outcome: found / partly_found / not_found / gave up
8. "Have you used the Ask tab or Ask Photos for this?"  *(ask-only; never explain; if they ask what it is: "Never mind, ignore it.")*

## C. Reverse-recall probe (10 min)
See `reverse_recall_probe_protocol.md` — the exact 5 steps, stopwatch, hard stop at 5 min.

## D. Workarounds and expectations (4 min)
1. "Besides Google Photos search, what else do you do to find old photos?"
2. "Imagine a friend who remembers every detail of your life sitting next to you. How would they help you find a half-remembered photo?"
3. "When you see a set of results, what makes you sure one of them is the right one?"

## E. Wrap-up (1 min)
- "Anything I didn't ask that matters?"

## Interviewer rules
- Never pitch or mention any solution/product idea.
- Never explain Google Photos features.
- Never lead ("Don't you think…", "you probably…").
- Silence is fine; wait 3–4 s after answers.
- Ask "Can you show me?" instead of "Do you…?"
- No names in notes; participant = P1…P6.
