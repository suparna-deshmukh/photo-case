# Interviewer Script — read this word-for-word (for friend-interviewers)

You are a student helping a research project on **how people find old photos on their phone**.
You will interview ONE person (about 25 minutes, on a call where they can share their phone screen in part C).
You are NOT selling anything. You are NOT testing a new app. You are just learning how people search for old photos.

## The 5 rules (memorise these before the call)
1. **Read the questions in the box exactly.** You may change "um" and "so" to sound natural, but do not change the meaning.
2. **Never give hints, never help, never explain how Google Photos works.** If they get stuck, say: *"Take your time."* and stay silent.
3. **Never say "don't you think…" or "you probably…".** Never agree loudly with their complaints. Say "Mm, go on." "Then what happened?" "Show me."
4. **Silence is a tool.** After they finish, wait 3-4 seconds before your next question. People add the interesting details in that pause.
5. **Write everything down** in the Note Sheet (open on another screen / paper). If you can write it down, just listen.

Timing: A = 2 min, B = 5 min, C = 10 min, D = 4 min, E = 1 min. If they run long in B, shorten E.

---

## OPENING (read, then pause)

> "Hi [NAME]! Thanks for making time. Quick thing before we start — I'm doing a small student research project. I want to understand how people look for old photos on their phones — like when you know a photo exists somewhere but you can't find it. It will take about 25 minutes on this call. It's completely voluntary, you can stop anytime, and in my report you'll only appear as 'P[number]' — no name, no personal details. Nothing you say will be shared with Google or any company. If you're comfortable with me recording the audio on my phone so I don't miss anything, I will — but you can tell me not to, and that's totally fine. In one part I'll ask you to share your phone screen while you search — and you can show only the search part, you don't have to show me the actual photos. Is that all okay for you?"

**[WAIT FOR YES. If no → do not proceed. If "yes but no recording" → continue without recording.]**
**[LOG: time started, participant code P1…P6]**

---

## SECTION A — Context (about 2 min)

**[READ]**
> "Okay, first a couple of simple questions. How long have you used Google Photos on your phone?"
> "And roughly how many photos and videos do you think you have in it — hundreds, a few thousand, more?"
> "Is it an Android phone or iPhone? And does it automatically back up photos, or do you upload sometimes?"

**[LOG: years of use, library size estimate, Android/iPhone, auto-backup yes/no → segment_flags]**
(If they say they use it only "sometimes", note it — they can still continue.)

---

## SECTION B — The last time you searched (about 5 min)

**[READ]**
> "Now the main part. Think about the last time you went looking for an old photo, video or screenshot that you knew existed. Could be anything — a screenshot, a travel photo, a document you had photographed. What were you trying to find?"

**[PAUSE and let them tell the story. Then, in this order:]**

**[READ]** "And why did you need it at that moment — what was it for?"
**[READ]** "What did you remember about that photo?"
*(after they list things, probe once only:)*
**[READ]** "How sure were you about each of those things — which part were you definitely sure of, and which part were you just guessing?"
**[LOG: each thing they remember → one line: type | value | sure/think-so. Types: person, place, time, what was happening, what it looks like, why they took it, text in it, where it came from.]**
**[READ]** "And what did you NOT remember, that you wished you did?"
**[LOG: forgotten things]**
**[READ]** "Okay — what was the first thing you typed or tapped, exactly?"
**[LOG: first action + exact words typed, if any]**
**[READ]** "And what happened next?"
*(follow with: "Then what did you do?" as many times as needed — this is where it gets interesting. Do not jump in with ideas.)*
**[READ]** "How did it end — did you find it or not?"
**[LOG: outcome — found / partly found / didn't find / gave up]**
**[READ]** "Last one on this: have you used the Ask tab or Ask Photos for this?"
*(Ask only. Do not explain what Ask Photos is. If they say "what's that?" just say: "Nothing, sorry — just forget it, I didn't ask.")*
**[LOG: used Ask tab yes/no/not stated]**

---

## SECTION C — Reverse-recall probe (about 10 min) — the most important part

**[READ exactly]**
> "Now I want to do a little test. Choose a photo or video from at least two years ago that you remember reasonably well — but not perfectly. Don't open Google Photos yet. Keep it in your head."

**[PAUSE 10 seconds. They may say they want to keep it private — that's fine.]**

**[READ]**
> "Now describe it to me, like you're explaining it to a friend. Say everything you remember — and for each thing, tell me whether you are sure or you just think so."

**[LOG EVERYTHING as rows: cue type | what they said | sure / think so | time. Keep writing while they talk.]**

**[READ]**
> "Okay, now share your screen on Google Photos and find it. And please think out loud — tell me what you're doing as you do it."

**[START STOPWATCH at their first tap. HARD STOP at 5 MINUTES — at 5 minutes say: "Okay, let's stop here." Do not help. Do not suggest queries. Do not say "have you tried…".]**
**[LOG in order:]**
- every query they type (copy the exact words)
- every tab or filter they use (Search / For you / Ask / Albums / map)
- scrolling vs typing (how much of each)
- number of reformulations (how many times they re-tried the search)
- whether they opened the Ask tab (yes/no)
- the moment they said they found it — or gave up

**[WHEN THEY FIND IT (or at the 5-minute stop), READ:]**
> "Looking at it now — what did you forget, or remember wrongly, compared to what you told me before?"
**[LOG each earlier cue as: correct / wrong / forgotten / can't verify]**

**[READ]**
> "Two quick scores. Before you started searching, how confident were you that you'd find it — 1 being 'no chance' to 5 being 'certain'?"
> "And now, how easy was the whole thing — 1 being 'very hard' to 7 being 'very easy'?"
**[LOG: confidence 1-5, ease 1-7. STOP STOPWATCH.]**

---

## SECTION D — Workarounds (about 4 min)

**[READ]**
> "Besides the search box in Google Photos, what else do you do when you can't find a photo?"
*(e.g., ask friends, check WhatsApp/Instagram, look in albums, scroll back in time — let them list them. Do not suggest any of these unless they're silent for 10 seconds.)*
**[LOG: workarounds mentioned, in their words]**

**[READ]**
> "Imagine a friend who remembers every single detail of your life is sitting next to you. They know where you were, who was with you, what you were wearing. How would that friend help you find a photo you only half-remember?"
**[LOG their answer — this is gold, write it near-verbatim]**

**[READ]**
> "When you get a screen full of results, what makes you sure that one of them is actually the right photo?"
**[LOG their answer]**

## SECTION E — Wrap-up (about 1 min)

**[READ]**
> "Last question: is there anything I didn't ask about finding old photos that you think matters?"
*(let them talk; then:)*
**[READ]**
> "That's everything, honestly — thank you so much, this was really helpful. I'll keep everything anonymous in the report. Nothing else needed from you."

**[LOG: time ended. Notable verbatim quote (one exact sentence they said that captured something).]**

## If things go wrong
- **They get stuck for a long time in C:** stay silent. If more than 90 seconds of silence, say only: *"Take your time."*
- **They ask "what should I type?":** *"I really don't want to influence you — what would you naturally type?"*
- **They refuse to show the photo itself:** *"That's fine — just show me what you're searching, you can look away from the photo."*
- **They mention their name/friends' names:** that's fine, you will not write names down.
- **Call drops:** restart with: *"Sorry it dropped — where were we, you were telling me about the photo from…"*
